Catalogs

The band you are in, not a folder.

A catalog holds its own songs, its own people and its own colours. Invite someone by email and they arrive with a colour of their own, so from their first line you can see what they added — and their face sits on the songs they started.

Not at the same time

The song moves while you are asleep.

Somebody adds a verse on a train, somebody else puts a lead on it at midnight, and it is all there in the morning. Songs with something on them you have not heard yet say so, so you know where to start — and every song has a conversation in it for the rest. There is no green dot and no "who is online": bands are not simultaneous, and pretending otherwise would just be a reason to feel late.

Quiet on purpose

You get told what needs you.

A take somebody played you have not heard is worth a nudge. Somebody editing a line is not — it would arrive once per line, and a writer who knows every word pings their band writes differently. So edits show up on the way in without ever interrupting anyone, and the inbox stays worth opening.

Gigs

Setlists that leave the app.

Build a set from the songs you have, reorder it, and print it or send it as a PDF. The people standing in for the night do not need an account to read it.

Credit

Who brought what, on the record.

Lines carry the colour of whoever wrote them and takes carry the name of whoever played them, from the moment they land. Not because anybody is keeping score, but because a year later nobody remembers, and the conversation about splits goes better when the answer is already written down.
