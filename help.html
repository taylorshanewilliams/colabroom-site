Stuck?

Tap the chord you're stuck on.

Every guitar app can show you a scale. None of them know your song. This one already does — the key, the chords, the bars, what you've played and what you haven't.

Where you are

That's the four chord.

Before anything else, it tells you where you're standing. A chord on its own is a shape. A chord in a key is a decision.

What else fits

Not a list of seven chords. The three you haven't used.

Anyone can look up what's in a key. Your song has used four of them. These are the ones it hasn't touched, and they'll fit without anything else changing.

For whatever you play

The same question, answered for your instrument.

A lead player wants the box. A bass player wants where the root can go. A singer wants the note that won't clash. Same chord, same song, three different answers — and you can see all of them, because most people play more than one thing.

Honest about what it knows

It won't make something up.

Play a chord from outside the key and it won't invent a number for it — borrowing is something you did on purpose. The harmony notes say they come from the chord, not from the tune, because the app heard your words and your chords, not your melody.

No waiting, no cost

It's arithmetic, not a guess.

There's one right answer to what fits in a key, and this works it out rather than predicting it. Nothing is sent anywhere, nothing is queued, and it works with no signal. Ask it forty times in a row on a train.

And when it can't help

Ask the room.

Some questions aren't arithmetic. Does this bridge work? Is the second chorus too long? The people you play with are already in here — the app suggests, a person decides.
