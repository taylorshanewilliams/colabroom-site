The Open Mic

Meet somebody
you haven't met.

A room is yours and your band's. The Open Mic is the one place you step onto on purpose — and step off again whenever you like.

Off until you say

Nothing here is public because you made it.

Being findable is a switch, and it starts off. Your songs stay in your room until you put one up. Three separate decisions can make something public, each one yours to take and yours to undo — and the app tells you which one you are making before you make it.

Asks

Say what the song needs.

Put a song where other musicians can hear it and name the thing it is missing — bass on the second verse. The ask carries the song with it: the audio, the key, the tempo, and what is already on it. Nobody has to guess what they are being asked for, and answering is one tap.

Listen

You decide by ear, in about ten seconds.

"Guitarist" does not tell you whether somebody plays metal or jazz. So the filter here is deliberately coarse — pick what you play, press Listen, and hear the songs that need it. A role is a filter, not a goal. What you are actually looking for is somebody you work well with, and that is heard, not filtered.

No ladder

Nobody is ranked.

There are no badges, no levels, no streaks and no chart of the most liked. Nobody is ordered by how good they are or how much they have done, because the moment a room does that it stops being a room. A phone recording carries a song into the Open Mic exactly as readily as a mastered mix.

Safe to accept

Report and block, on everything.

Every place somebody else's music, picture or message appears carries a way to report it and a way to block the person who put it there. Blocking also closes anything already open between the two of you. What arrives from a stranger has not been reviewed before you see it, and the app says so.

An honest empty

A quiet noticeboard is a healthy one.

The Open Mic is a noticeboard, not a feed. Nothing scrolls forever and nothing is there to keep you here. Some days nobody needs anything, and that is what it looks like when a room is small and new rather than broken.
