Why anybody bothers

    
The singer drops it a tone and the chart doesn't change.

    

      That is the whole thing. On a normal chart, moving a song for somebody's
      voice makes every letter on the page wrong. On a number chart, 1 4 5 is
      still 1 4 5 — the band works out the letters on the way to the microphone.
      It is why a session player would rather be handed numbers.
    

  


  

    
How to read one

    
Count the chords of the key, one to seven.

    

      In G, that is G A B C D E F♯ — so G is 1, C is 4 and D is 5. A minor chord
      gets a dash after the number, which is why Am in G is 2-. Anything from
      outside the key carries a flat or a sharp in front: the F in a G song is
      ♭7. Roman numerals say the same thing with the quality in the case.
    

  


  

    
Minor songs

    
There are two right answers.

    

      Chas Williams' The Nashville Number System — the book the whole
      thing is written down in — builds every chart on the major scale, so a
      song in A minor is counted against C and its home chord is 6-. Plenty of
      players who mostly read minor material count from the minor tonic instead
      and read the same song as 1- ♭7 ♭6 ♭3. Both are on this page and neither
      is the advanced one. Roman numerals don't have the argument: they always
      count a minor key from its own tonic.
