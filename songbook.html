The song sheet

A page, not a form.

You type a song the way you would write it on paper. Everything else — who wrote which line, where the chords go — happens around you.

Who wrote what

Everyone's colour, on their own lines.

Months later you can still see who brought which line, without anybody having to remember. Tap the dot beside a line to leave a voice note on it — the melody you cannot write down, kept where the words are.

We drove out past the county line The radio was playing something Neither of us knew the words So we made them up instead
Chords

Chords where the chords go.

Above the word they land on, not in a separate column you have to read sideways. Transpose the whole song if the singer needs it lower, then print it or send a PDF to whoever is standing in tonight.

Stuck on a chord

Tap it. It shows you how to play it.

Every chord on the sheet is a tap target, because a sheet gets read by people who cannot yet play everything on it. Tap one for the shape — the open position where there is one, otherwise the nearest barre with the fret named — the notes in it, what a bass player does with it, and the five notes that work over it.

The key

In your key, not an example key.

Tap the key on the sheet for its scale, the chords that belong to it — each one tappable through to its own shape — and where to put a capo to play it in open chords. Nothing here is a reference table you have to transpose in your head. The song already knows its key, so the answer arrives already in it.

On stage

It moves at the speed of the song.

Live mode scrolls the sheet while you play, with a count-in and a tempo you set — so the words are where your eyes are, and your hands stay on the instrument.

Bring what you have

Songs do not have to start here.

Import from a PDF, a spreadsheet, a Google Sheets link, a text file, or just paste it in. You see exactly what will be added before anything lands in the song.
